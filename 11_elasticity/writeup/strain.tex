First, we need to apply a volume conserving increase of 2\% ($k=1.02$) to the c/a ratio. Apply the constraints $\frac{c_f}{a_f}=k\cdot \frac{c_0}{a_0}$, ${a_f}{b_f}c_f=a_0b_0c_0$, and $\frac{b_f}{a_f} = \frac{b_0}{a_0}$ to the cell-volume equation $V=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}abc$, and we find that: \[a_f = k^{-1/3}a_0,\ b_f = k^{-1/3}b_0,\ c_f = k^{2/3}c_0\]
Applying this to our CIF for hcp-scandium and plugging into the Bilbao strain tool yields:
\[\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = 
\begin{Bmatrix}
    -0.006588 & 0 & 0 \\
    0 & -0.006588 & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & 0.013288 \\
\end{Bmatrix}    
\]
This aligns with intuition that, when applying a strain along the c-axis, the b-a plane surface area must shrink by the inverse factor to perserve volume.
